Dueday: landing page

A full landing page for an invented bill-reminder app, written and built as a working website.

Open the live landing page

Overview

A complete landing page: hero, problem, benefits, features, how it works, FAQ and a final call to action. I wrote the copy and built the page so you can see how the words work in a real layout.

Objective

Get a visitor to try the app, by making the problem familiar and the solution feel easy.

Target audience

Adults who manage their own bills and have missed a due date at least once.

Brand voice

Calm, practical and slightly dry. It treats a forgotten bill as a normal human mistake, not a character flaw.

Key copy

Stop paying late fees for bills you forgot.

Dueday keeps every due date in one place and nudges you before each one. Setup takes about five minutes.

Button: Try Dueday free

The rest of the copy lives on the page itself.

Strategy

Headline. It names the pain in the reader's own terms (late fees, forgotten bills), then the subheadline explains the solution in one sentence.

Order of sections. Problem first, so the reader nods. Then the solution, the proof of how it works, then objections in the FAQ, then one last ask. Each section answers the question a sceptical visitor would have next.

One action. Every button says the same thing, so the visitor is never choosing between goals.

No social proof. I left the testimonial section out on purpose. Invented reviews would undermine the honesty of the portfolio, even if labelled.

Skills demonstrated

Conversion-focused writing, page structure, headline and subheadline writing, objection handling in FAQs, and basic HTML/CSS.
