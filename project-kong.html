Chill & Chew Kit

Packaging copy for an invented KONG product: a freezable stuffing kit for medium dogs.

Overview

A practice brief to write front-of-pack and back-of-pack copy for a product that doesn't exist. I based the voice on how KONG describes itself publicly. Product facts below are invented for the exercise.

Objective

Make a first-time dog owner pick the box up in about three seconds, then give them enough on the back to feel confident buying it.

Target audience

First-time owners of a medium-sized dog who want something to keep the dog busy, and who read the pack in a shop aisle, standing up.

Brand voice

Warm, a little playful, specific. It talks like a friendly person who knows dogs, not like a product spec sheet. It never promises results it can't back up.

Copy

Front of pack

KONG

Chill & Chew Kit

Freezable stuffing kit for medium dogs

Fill it. Freeze it. Let them dig in.

  • 100% natural rubber
  • Dishwasher-safe (top rack)
  • Recipe card inside

Back of pack

About

One medium rubber toy and a card of three stuffing ideas. Fill, freeze, hand over. Your dog gets something to work on, and you get to watch them figure it out.

Why you'll like it

  • Gives your dog something to lick, chew and puzzle over
  • Three easy recipes to start, no special ingredients
  • Washes clean, so it's ready for the next round

How to use it

  1. Fill with your dog's usual food or a dog-safe filling (not included).
  2. Freeze until solid.
  3. Hand it over, and stay nearby while they enjoy it.

Care

Wash after each use, or pop it in the top rack of the dishwasher.

[Final safety and legal wording would come from the brand's compliance team.]

More stuffing ideas: kong.example/stuffing-ideas

Strategy

Front panel hierarchy. A shopper gets about three seconds, so the order is brand, what it is, one line of reason to care, then three quick proof points. Everything else moved to the back.

The tagline. Three short commands that match the three actions of the product. It's easy to say aloud and shows exactly how the kit works.

Claims I avoided. I left out words like "indestructible" and anything about stopping bad behaviour. The product can't prove those, and on pet packaging they're risky. The benefits describe what the dog does (lick, chew, puzzle), not what the product fixes.

Back panel. Someone holding the box has already shown interest, so the back answers three questions in order: what is it, why would I want it, how do I use it. Safety wording is left as a marked placeholder, because that belongs to the brand's compliance team, not the copywriter.

Skills demonstrated

Packaging hierarchy, brand voice matching, benefits over features, writing within tight space, compliance awareness, proofreading.
