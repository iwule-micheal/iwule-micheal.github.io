Fernlow: Cedar & Fig Soy Candle

E-commerce product copy for an invented candle brand.

Overview

A product page written the way a small online shop would use it: title, short description, full description, features and care.

Objective

Help a shopper picture the candle, trust the details, and add it to the basket.

Target customer

Adults buying for their own home or as a gift, who read product pages properly before spending on something small but personal.

Brand voice

Quiet and sensory. Plain words, concrete details, no inflated claims.

Copy

Cedar & Fig Soy Candle, 220 g

£24

Short description

A soy wax candle that smells like fresh-cut cedar and ripe fig, in a glass jar you can reuse. Burns for up to 45 hours.

Full description

Light it and the room slowly fills with warm cedar, then fig leaf, then a soft edge of vanilla. It's a scent for evenings, not for covering up the kitchen.

We pour each candle by hand in small batches, using soy wax and a cotton wick for a steady, even burn. When the wax is finished, wash out the jar and use it for pencils, cotton pads or a small plant.

Key features

  • 220 g of soy wax, up to 45 hours of burn time
  • Cotton wick
  • Scent notes: cedar, fig leaf, soft vanilla
  • Reusable glass jar with lid

Why you'll like it

  • Fills a living room or bedroom without overpowering it
  • Looks plain and good on a shelf, so it works as a gift
  • The jar gets a second life

Burn tips

Trim the wick to 5 mm before each burn. Burn for 2 to 3 hours at a time so the wax melts evenly. Never leave a burning candle unattended.

Add to basket (button)

Strategy

Title. It names the scent and the size, which is what shoppers search for and compare.

Short description. It gives the scent, the container and the burn time in two sentences, for people who scan.

Full description. Candles are bought on the senses, and customers can't smell a screen. So the copy walks through the scent in the order you'd notice it. "Not for covering up the kitchen" sets honest expectations.

Features and benefits. The feature list holds the facts (grams, hours, wick). The benefits list says what those facts mean for the buyer.

Honesty. "Up to 45 hours" is a ceiling, not a promise. I made no health or "clean burning" claims, and the safety line stays in.

Skills demonstrated

Sensory writing, features versus benefits, scannable page structure, honest claims, product page conventions.
